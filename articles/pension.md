# The pension calculator

Take the lump sum, or the monthly benefit? The calculator answers with one rate: the return the lump sum would have to earn, every year, to pay out what the pension pays. If that return is above what the household expects its investments to earn, the pension is the better deal; below, the lump sum is.

## On life expectancy

Nobody knows how long they will live, so each monthly payment is counted by the chance of being alive to receive it, from SSA's period life table (Period Life Table, 2023, as used in the 2026 Trustees Report), by each person's biological sex. Without a sex entered, the average of the men's and women's tables is used. The payments start at the start age, so the table is read from there: the owner is alive when the pension begins.

The page shows the expected return on that basis, the expected payments in all, and life expectancy at the start age (for example 19.3 more years at 65, men and women averaged, to about 84).

## A survivor benefit

For a couple, a survivor share (50%, 75% or 100%) continues to the spouse after the owner's death. Each survivor payment is counted by the chance the spouse is alive and the owner isn't, the two lifetimes taken as independent.

## Cost-of-living increases

When the pension rises each year (1% to 3%), the payments step up once a year from the start.

## How long you live decides it

The table under the result shows the same return if the payments ran to exactly 75, 80, 85, 90, 95 or 100: a short life favors the lump sum, a long one the pension. With a survivor share, each row's survivor payments run to the spouse's life expectancy.

## Against your assumed return

The household's assumed return is after inflation, while a pension pays in the dollars of the day, so it is compared at the assumed return plus inflation (7% after 2.5% inflation is about 9.7%). The page also shows what the expected payments are worth today at that return, beside the lump sum offered, and the age at which the payments received add up to the lump sum (no interest).

## What it leaves out

Tax is left out because both options are taxed alike: a lump sum rolled into a Pre-tax account and a pension are both ordinary income when they are paid out. Not modeled: the plan's own financial strength, and the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation's limits on what it insures.
